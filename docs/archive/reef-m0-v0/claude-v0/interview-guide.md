# Interview guide

25 minutes. The participant should talk for at least 20 of them.

---

## Rules

1. **Never name the problem.** Not "is document review painful?" — ask what they did and
   how long it took. If you supply the pain, they will confirm it.
2. **Past, not future.** "What did you do on your last deal" is data. "Would you use
   something that…" is fiction. Never ask a hypothetical about behavior.
3. **One deal, in detail.** Specifics beat generalities. A person describing their
   process in general describes an idealized version they do not follow.
4. **Silence.** After an answer, wait. The second thing they say is usually the real one.
5. **Do not pitch.** Not at the end either, until the commitment ladder. The moment they
   know you're selling, the data degrades.
6. **Numbers, always.** "A while" → "How many hours, roughly?" Push once, accept a range.
7. **Write quotes verbatim.** Paraphrase loses the phrasing that becomes your copy.

**Banned phrasings**, because each contains its own answer:

> ~~"Do you find it hard to keep track of everything in a data room?"~~
> ~~"Would it be valuable to have every finding linked to its source?"~~
> ~~"How much time do you waste on document review?"~~
> ~~"Would you pay for something that saved you 40 hours?"~~

**Replacements:**

> "Walk me through the last data room you opened. What did you do first?"
> "How did you keep track of what you'd read?"
> "Which parts took the longest?"
> "What did the last one you did cost you, all in?"

## Opening — 2 min

> Thanks for the time. I'm researching how people actually run document diligence on
> smaller acquisitions. I'm not selling anything today and there's nothing to show you.
> I mostly want to hear about your most recent deal in detail. Fine to skip anything
> confidential, and I won't name you or the company in anything I write up.
>
> Can I record this so I'm not typing while you talk?

Then, immediately, the qualifier:

> Before we start — when did you last personally open a data room and read the documents
> yourself?

Anything over 12 months, or "my analyst does that," politely reroute to the analyst.

---

## Core — 18 min

Ask about **one specific real deal.** If they start generalizing, pull them back: "Let's
stay on that one — what happened next?"

### 1 · Arrival

- How did that opportunity come to you?
- What did you get first, before the full room?
- What made you decide to go deeper rather than pass?
- How long between first look and LOI?

*Listening for:* whether an earlier, cheaper, shallower screening product is the real
opportunity. If they pass on twenty deals for every one they pursue, the screen is a
higher-frequency job than the deep read.

### 2 · The room

- Where did the documents live? Who controlled it?
- Roughly how many files? What was the mix?
- How was it organized when you got it?
- How much was scanned versus native?
- Did documents keep arriving after you started?

*Listening for:* F1 (batch vs. trickle), T1 (scan ratio), and whether the incumbent VDR
is a competitor or a neutral container.

### 3 · The reading

- Walk me through the first day. What did you open first?
- How did you decide what to read and what to skim?
- How did you track what you'd covered?
- Who else read documents? How did you split it?
- What did you do when something referenced a document you didn't have?

*Listening for:* the tracking artifact. Almost everyone has a spreadsheet, and that
spreadsheet is the shape the memo must fit into.

### 4 · Time

- Roughly how many hours did you personally spend reading documents?
- Over how many calendar days?
- What was the single most time-consuming part?
- Was there anything you'd have skipped if you'd had less time?

*Listening for:* the real hour count. Push for a range. Then ask what it displaced —
"what didn't you get to?" — which measures the true cost better than the hours do.

### 5 · Risk and near-misses

- Was there anything you found late that changed the price or the terms?
- Anything you found *after* closing that you wish you'd caught?
- Have you heard of that happening to someone else?
- What are you most worried about missing?

*Listening for:* the actual checklist. The things people volunteer here are what the
memo must lead with. Third-party stories are useful — people describe others' disasters
more freely than their own.

### 6 · Outputs and handoffs

- What did you produce out of all that reading?
- Who read it?
- What format? Did anyone push back on it?
- What did you outsource — QoE, legal, insurance, environmental?
- What did *they* deliver, and how did it overlap with your own work?

*Listening for:* **F4, the most important question in the guide.** If the QoE report and
the attorney's issues list already cover most of the checklist, Reef's space is the
narrow middle and the price is much lower than $1,500.

### 7 · Tools and money

- What software did you use during that deal?
- What do you pay for annually that touched this process?
- Who approves that spend?
- Did you consider anything and reject it? Why?

*Listening for:* W2, the budget owner, and the real anchor price in this market.

### 8 · Frequency and permission

- How many deals have you taken to this depth in the last 12 months?
- How many do you expect in the next 12?
- When you sign an NDA with a seller, does it restrict sharing documents with outside
  service providers?
- Have you ever had to get seller consent to bring in a vendor?

*Listening for:* **N1/N3 and A2.** Do not leave the call without an answer to the NDA
question. It is a legal fact with a binary answer that determines whether the product is
deliverable at all, and it takes forty seconds to ask.

## Role-specific paths

Substitute for section 6 when the participant is not the buyer.

### Search-fund principal — in search
- How many LOIs have you signed? How many died in diligence, and what killed them?
- Who pays for diligence — you, or your investors, and does that change what you'll spend?
- What does your investor update look like during a live deal?

### Search-fund principal — recently closed
- Six months in, what's in the business you didn't know at close?
- If you had those hours back, where would you have spent them?
- Would you have paid to find that thing earlier? How much?

### Analyst / associate
- What did you get handed and what were you told to produce?
- What was the most repetitive part?
- What did you build to make it easier — spreadsheet, template, anything?
- What got reviewed by someone senior, and what did they change?

### Operating partner
- When you get pulled in, what do you read?
- What do the deal people consistently miss that you catch?

### Lender
- How many acquisition files do you review a year?
- What documents must be in the file before it can go to credit?
- What's the most common reason a file comes back incomplete?
- Who assembles it, and what do you do when something's missing?

*This path matters most.* A lender touches every deal in the ecosystem and has the
frequency the searcher lacks.

### QoE accountant
- What's in scope for a QoE and what do buyers wrongly assume is covered?
- What do you tell them to check themselves?
- How many engagements a year? How is it priced?
- Would you white-label a document review for your clients, or does it conflict?

*The competitive question, asked directly and neutrally.* Their answer determines
whether they are a channel, a customer, or a competitor.

### Transaction attorney
- What's in your issues list, and what's explicitly out?
- What do buyers most often get wrong about what you're reviewing?
- Under a standard NDA, can a buyer run documents through a third-party service?
- Does anything change if the tool doesn't retain the data?

## Commitment ladder — 5 min

Only now, and only ascending. Stop at the first refusal and note where it came; **the
rung where they stop is the finding.**

**1 · Materials**
> Would you be willing to share an anonymized or redacted version of a room you've
> already closed, so I can see what the documents actually look like?

*Tests A1. Free, low-risk, and a refusal here is a strong early signal.*

**2 · Concierge test**
> If I ran a real review on a live room of yours and handed you a findings memo in three
> days, would you want that on your next deal?

*Tests desirability with delivery attached. Still cheap for them.*

**3 · Referral**
> Who else should I be talking to? Would you introduce me?

*Tests whether the problem is worth talking about. Unsolicited intros are the strongest
non-monetary signal available.*

**4 · Pilot agreement**
> I'm running three paid pilots this month at $1,500 per deal. Want one of the slots?

*Tests W1. Note the exact reaction to the number — hesitation, surprise, or "that's it?"
are three completely different findings. Record which.*

**5 · Deposit**
> I can hold a slot with a $500 deposit, refundable if the memo isn't useful.

*Tests W3, and it is the only rung that is actual evidence. Everything below it is
conversation.*

If they stop at 4 but not 5, the finding is not "they said yes" — it is that stated
willingness did not survive contact with a payment form.

## After every interview, same day

Fifteen minutes, non-negotiable, while it's fresh:

1. Three verbatim quotes, exact wording
2. Hours spent on their last deal — the number
3. Deals in the last 12 months — the number
4. The NDA answer — yes / no / unsure
5. The tracking artifact they described
6. Highest ladder rung reached
7. **One thing that contradicted the hypothesis.** If there isn't one, you were probably
   leading, and the transcript should be reread for where.

Item 7 is the discipline that makes the rest worth anything.

Update `workflow-map.md` after every third interview, not at the end. Patterns that
emerge at interview six change what you ask at interview seven.

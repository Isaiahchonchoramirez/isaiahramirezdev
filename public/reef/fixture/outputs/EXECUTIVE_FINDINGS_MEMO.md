## The five things that matter

**1 · The largest customer is more than twice the size you were told, and has a consent
right over this transaction.**
Lakeside Steel is 22.4% of FY2025 revenue, not the 11% stated in the Q&A. Its master
service agreement deems a change of control an assignment requiring prior written
consent, withholdable at the customer's sole discretion, with a 30-day termination right.
These two facts are individually significant and jointly the largest item in the package.
*R-01, R-02*

**2 · The fixed charge coverage covenant is not met on the seller's own calculation.**
1.18x against a 1.25x minimum, on the compliance certificate in the package. No waiver or
amendment was supplied. *R-03*

**3 · The Erie facility is being held over on an expired lease.**
It expired 2025-11-30 with no renewal option. The Q&A and the management meeting both
state all leases run through 2028 or later. Erie is 3.8M of revenue. *R-05*

**4 · Thirteen field personnel are paid on 1099 while the Q&A states all field staff are
employees.**
Ten are titled "Field technician." The headcount reconciles — 71 W-2 plus 13 contractors
equals the 84-person roster — so this is a classification question, not a counting error.
*Classification is a legal and tax determination and is not addressed here.* *R-07*

**5 · Recurring revenue is 78% or 52% depending on the definition, and the definition is
not stated where the number is used.**
Both are arithmetically correct. Management counts time-and-materials work from
contract-holding customers as recurring. This is a definition difference, not a
misstatement — but the two figures support materially different valuations. *R-04*
